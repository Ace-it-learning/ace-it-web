# Writing Genre Learning Content Proposals

Below is the proposed pedagogical content for the 12 Writing Genres. This data will be used to populate the Learning Guides (landing pages) in the student portal.

## 1. Debate Speech (`writing_genre_debate`)
- **Format**: 
  - Salutation: "Honorable Chairman, ladies and gentlemen..." or "Good morning/afternoon, honorable judges, opponents and members of the audience."
  - Stance: Immediate and clear declaration (e.g., "I am here today to speak strongly in favor of...")
  - Closing: "Thank you."
- **Tips**: 
  - Use signposting words (e.g., "My first/second/third point is...")
  - Include rhetorical questions to engage the audience.
  - End with a summary call-to-action.
- **Traps**: 
  - Forgetting the audience—debate is a PERFORMANCE, not just an essay.
  - Overly aggressive tone—maintain "passionate but professional."
- **Micro-skills**: Persuasive language, Signposting, Rebuttal techniques.

## 2. Letter to the Editor (`writing_genre_lte`)
- **Format**:
  - Salutation: "Dear Editor,"
  - Opening: Purpose of writing (e.g., "I am writing to express my concern regarding...")
  - Closing: "Yours faithfully, [Name]"
- **Tips**:
  - Use a formal, objective tone.
  - Structure logic: Problem -> Cause -> Impact -> Suggestion.
  - Cite recent news or local context to show relevance.
- **Traps**:
  - Using informal language or slang.
  - Being purely negative—always offer constructive solutions.
- **Micro-skills**: Formal register, Logical development, Public discourse tone.

## 3. Expository Essay (`writing_genre_exp`)
- **Format**:
  - Title (optional but recommended).
  - Introduction: Hook + background + thesis statement.
  - Conclusion: Restated thesis + summary of main points + final thought.
- **Tips**:
  - Focus on "What", "Why", and "How".
  - Use a neutral, authoritative voice.
  - Provide specific examples (facts, data, scenarios).
- **Traps**:
  - Turning it into an argumentative essay (staying neutral vs taking a side).
  - Lack of clear paragraphing.
- **Micro-skills**: Objective description, Evidence-based writing, Topic sentences.

## 4. Short Story (`writing_genre_fic`)
- **Format**:
  - Title.
  - Narrative Arc: Exposition -> Rising Action -> Climax -> Resolution.
- **Tips**:
  - "Show, Don't Tell"—use descriptive adjectives and adverbs.
  - Build tension through pacing.
  - Use dialogue sparingly but effectively to reveal character.
- **Traps**:
  - Too much plot, not enough emotion or atmosphere.
  - Weak or rushed endings.
- **Micro-skills**: Descriptive language, Character development, Narrative pacing.

## 5. Personal Experience (`writing_genre_per`)
- **Format**:
  - First-person perspective ("I", "my").
  - Reflection: What happened -> How you felt -> What you learned.
- **Tips**:
  - Use a range of "internal" verbs (e.g., "I realized", "I pondered").
  - Connect the personal event to a broader life lesson.
- **Traps**:
  - Being too "blog-like" or informal—maintain DSE linguistic standards.
  - Listing events without any emotional reflection.
- **Micro-skills**: Reflective tone, Emotional vocabulary, Sentence variety.

## 6. Biographical Profile (`writing_genre_bio`)
- **Format**:
  - Heading (Subject's name).
  - Structure: Early life -> Achievements -> Impact on others -> Legacy.
- **Tips**:
  - Use chronological order.
  - Use "Inspirational" language.
  - Highlight the "Human Element"—challenges they overcame.
- **Traps**:
  - Just listing dates/facts (Wikipedia-style).
  - Forgetting to explain *why* this person is noteworthy.
- **Micro-skills**: Chronological cohesion, Praiseworthy register, Summarization.

## 7. Formal Letter (`writing_genre_fml`)
- **Format**:
  - Headers: Sender's addr (Top L/R), Recipient's addr (L), Date.
  - Salutation: "Dear Mr. [Name]," or "Dear Sir/Madam,"
  - Closing: "Yours sincerely" (known) / "Yours faithfully" (unknown).
- **Tips**:
  - Get straight to the point in paragraph 1.
  - Use the "You" approach—focus on the recipient's needs/situation.
- **Traps**:
  - Mixed register—starting formal and slipping into casual.
  - Inappropriate salutation/closing pairings.
- **Micro-skills**: Formal conventions, Professional tone, Transactional clarity.

## 8. Report (`writing_genre_rpt`)
- **Format**:
  - Subject line (e.g., "Report on...").
  - To / From / Date.
  - Sub-headings: Introduction, Findings, Recommendations, Conclusion.
- **Tips**:
  - Use statistics or "observed trends".
  - Use the passive voice (e.g., "It was observed that...") for objectivity.
  - Bullet points are acceptable for findings.
- **Traps**:
  - Using too many "I thinks"—keep it factual and based on findings.
  - Poor organization—not using sub-headings clearly.
- **Micro-skills**: Information synthesis, Sectional labeling, Objective reporting.

## 9. Proposal (`writing_genre_prp`)
- **Format**:
  - Heading: "Proposal for...".
  - Sections: Introduction/Purpose, Rationale, Proposed Activities, Budget/Timeline, Benefits.
- **Tips**:
  - Focus on Persuasion—why should the reader say YES?
  - Be specific with details (dates, costs, roles).
- **Traps**:
  - Vague ideas—"We should have a party" (Bad) vs "A 3-hour Cultural Festival on Dec 15" (Good).
  - Forgetting the "Future Benefit" section.
- **Micro-skills**: Persuasive justification, Future-tense usage, Specificity.

## 10. Review (`writing_genre_rev`)
- **Format**:
  - Title (e.g., "A Must-See Musical").
  - Intro: The "What/Where/When".
  - Body: Pros, Cons, Comparative analysis.
  - Conclusion: Final rating/recommendation.
- **Tips**:
  - Use "Evaluative Adjectives" (e.g., "underwhelming", "riveting", "pioneering").
  - Provide a balanced view (even if you love it, find one thing to improve).
- **Traps**:
  - Just summarizing the plot/content without EVALUATING it.
  - Too much slang.
- **Micro-skills**: Evaluative language, Comparative structures, Audience guidance.

## 11. Feature Article (`writing_genre_art`)
- **Format**:
  - Catchy Headline.
  - Byline (e.g., "By Chris Wong").
  - Lead paragraph: Grabs attention immediately.
- **Tips**:
  - Use a "Lively" and "Engaging" tone.
  - Use direct address ("You might wonder...") and quotes (even if fictional).
- **Traps**:
  - Boring headline.
  - Too much formality—it should read like a magazine, not a textbook.
- **Micro-skills**: Journalistic tone, Lead-writing, Engagement devices.

## 12. Personal Letter/Email (`writing_genre_let`)
- **Format**:
  - Salutation: "Hi [Name]," or "Dear [Name],"
  - Opening: Friendly chat (e.g., "Hope you're doing well!")
  - Closing: "Best," "Cheers," "Love," [Name].
- **Tips**:
  - Use contractions (e.g., "won't", "can't") for a natural feel.
  - Ask questions back to the recipient to show interaction.
- **Traps**:
  - Being TOO informal (slang that an examiner wouldn't understand).
  - Forgetting the specific purpose given in the prompt (stay on topic).
- **Micro-skills**: Semi-formal register, Conversational flow, Question-tag usage.
